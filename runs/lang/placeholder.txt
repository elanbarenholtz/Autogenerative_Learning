The river moved slowly through the valley, carrying with it the quiet memory of the mountains. In the morning the light fell across the water and the stones beneath it seemed to shift, though they had not moved in a thousand years. A traveler walked along the bank, thinking of the road behind and the road ahead, and of how little the two had in common. The wind carried the smell of rain that had not yet arrived. Birds rose from the reeds and turned together in the air, a single body made of many wings, and then they scattered and were ordinary again. The traveler stopped to rest beneath a tree whose roots had grown around a stone, holding it the way a hand holds a small and stubborn thing. There was no hurry now. The town was far, and the day was long, and the river would arrive before he did, as rivers always do. He thought about the people he had known, and the ones he had only imagined, and found that he could no longer tell them apart. This did not trouble him. The water spoke in a language older than speech, and he had spent his life learning to listen without understanding, which is the only honest way to listen at all.
